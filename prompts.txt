Prompts.txt provides a detailed overview of my experience using an AI tool
(ChatGPT) during the development of my term project.
I break down the best and worst parts of the interaction across various phases:
Brainstorming, Development, Testing & Debugging, and Finalizing.

========================================================================
Phase 1: Brainstorming & Planning
========================================================================
Best Parts:
- Initial Idea Generation:
  I started with two ideas—a 2048-style game and a rhythm game using my own music. I used AI to quickly
  outline the pros and cons of both concepts. A prompt like, “Here are my ideas for my Java game; can you develop
  a timeline needed to accomplish my goals within the next month?” generated a detailed, day-by-day schedule.

- Clear Timeline Creation:
  The AI was surprisingly effective at breaking down the month into manageable tasks, enabling me to set short-term
  milestones. It helped me refine my requirements and provided structure to the project from the very beginning.

- Project Structure Assistance:
  When I was uncertain about how to plan all the classes needed for my custom game, the AI helped me draft a basic
  project structure. By feeding it detailed requirements and referencing class relationships,
  I quickly generated an outline that became the blueprint for the game.

- Checklist Development:
  The iterative conversation with the AI resulted in a comprehensive checklist of features and requirements.
  This checklist was valuable in keeping my project organized and ensuring I did not overlook any critical aspect.

Worst Parts:
- Limited Context Retention:
  Although the AI helped initially, its small context window meant I had to reintroduce previous information
  multiple times. This was particularly frustrating when trying to maintain continuity in the brainstorming phase.

- Dependency on Detailed Prompts:
  While detailed prompts yielded better results, it also meant spending extra time crafting precise questions.
  A slight vagueness would lead to less useful output, requiring multiple revisions to get the intended detail.

========================================================================
Phase 2: Development
========================================================================
Best Parts:
- Concept Explanations and Tutoring:
  The AI served as a rapid tutor, quickly explaining JavaFX concepts and other technical details. This saved me
  hours of searching for tutorials and allowed me to get back on track with minimal downtime.

- Structural Guidance in Code:
  When I needed guidance on implementing complex logic—such as drawing pipelines to connect nodes, the AI was useful
  in pointing out holes in my logic. It suggested strategies to simplify the process, helping me rework my approach.

- Quick Brainstorming for Revisions:
  Faced with a design challenge (like incorporating a “twist” in the rhythm game), I spent three days discussing
  ideas with the AI. Although progress was slow, the brainstorming sessions helped me evaluate different directions
  before switching to a new project concept (Resource Router).

Worst Parts:
- Inconsistent Code Quality:
  The AI often provided pseudocode that looked promising on paper but failed miserably in implementation.
  For example, its detailed summary for combining color logic failed 80% of the time when executed,
  forcing me to rework the logic entirely.

- Frustration with Limited Persistence:
  Constantly having to re-prompt the AI with previous work was a major roadblock. This lack of memory disrupted
  my workflow and often led to delays, as I had to invest time in re-establishing context.

- Poor Styling Suggestions:
  Despite strict guidelines, the AI’s code styling suggestions were consistently poor. I ended up spending hours
  manually fixing and optimizing the styling, which detracted from the development progress.

========================================================================
Phase 3: Testing, Debugging & Finalizing
========================================================================
Best Parts:
- Targeted Debugging Assistance:
  During testing, the AI helped me pinpoint where certain pieces of logic were located within my code.
  This was particularly useful when I couldn’t remember how a specific section was implemented.

- Innovative Testing Solutions:
  I encountered challenges testing abstract classes, particularly for the GameNode class.
  The AI offered a detailed walkthrough on creating a “Dummy” class for testing purposes,
  which was one of its few consistent successes.

- Efficiency in Problem Identification:
  In some cases, when error states were misfiring or components were not connecting correctly
  (such as circles only connecting to one square), the AI’s suggestions led to quicker identification of
  issues—allowing for faster fixes.

Worst Parts:
- Ineffective UI Debugging:
  AI support for JavaFX-related issues was virtually unhelpful. On multiple occasions, its recommendations led me
  in circles without resolving the problem. This was especially problematic when I needed to address error states
  triggering at inappropriate times.

- Scope Creep:
  As my project evolved, the AI’s explanations and tutorial suggestions sometimes introduced new problems or
  expanded the scope unnecessarily. This resulted in wasted time and increased frustration, leading me to
  eventually abandon AI for final fixes.

- Lack of Context in Complex Debugging:
  When debugging intricate issues, such as parsing level files and maintaining recipes for level creation,
  the AI often forgot earlier parts of the conversation. This lack of sustained context forced me to spend
  extra time re-establishing background information.

========================================================================
Final Reflections & Overall Advantages/Disadvantages
========================================================================
Advantages:
- Effective Planning and Timeline Tool: AI helped me establish a clear, actionable schedule for my project.

- Useful for Brainstorming: It provided a wide range of ideas and refined my project’s structure,
  which was essential during the early phases.

- Helpful Explanations: Acting as a personal tutor, the AI clarified difficult concepts like JavaFX,
  which accelerated my learning.

- Problem-Solving Guidance: Its insights into my code logic often pointed out areas for improvement,
  aiding in refining complex functionalities.

- Rapid Adjustments: In debugging, AI’s quick pointers allowed me to make targeted changes swiftly.

Disadvantages:
- Limited Context and Memory: Repeated re-prompting was required, resulting in delays and fragmented development.

- Unreliable Code Generation: The quality of code output was inconsistent, often needing extensive rework.

- Poor Code Styling: AI-generated styling suggestions were suboptimal, leading to significant manual rework.

- Difficulty with Advanced Explanations: The AI struggled with deeper, nuanced explanations required
  for advanced features.

- Inconsistent UI Debugging: Its inability to effectively troubleshoot JavaFX issues was a major drawback.

- Scope Creep: Over-reliance on AI for iterative suggestions sometimes expanded the project scope unnecessarily.

- Increased Frustration: The combination of context loss and unreliable outputs occasionally hindered
  progress rather than helping it.

========================================================================
Conclusion
========================================================================
Overall, my experience with AI was a mixed bag. While it excelled as a brainstorming partner and provided
efficient planning and rapid troubleshooting in some cases, its limitations—especially in code quality,
context retention, and UI debugging—presented significant challenges. The interaction with AI was a powerful tool
when used carefully and aided by my own critical thinking.
